### What B1 shows

- **The DEVSIM post-solve current becomes exact to float64 resolution.**
  - In double precision, `get_contact_current` has an absolute error of 2.4e-12 to 1.8e-11 A/cm on these meshes. At AR-1 that is 4.6e3 times the drain current at 1 mV.
  - With the three flags, the drain current is bit-identical to the circuit-node current in 4 of 6 runs. Elsewhere it differs by ≤ 2.5e-29 A/cm.
  - The source-side KCL error is at most 2.5 units in the last place of the largest returned current (D3), that is, at float64 resolution.
  - The source contact current is repaired the same way (D5, post hoc). Its G_SD Richardson value missed the network by 0.94 to 1.01 at AR-1 to AR-5 and by 6.4e-4 at AR-6 in double precision. In extended precision it is within 2.5e-10.
- **Mechanism, from DEVSIM's source** (`e3a25f0`, `src/math/ExternalPreconditioner.cc`). Models, equations and the Newton residual are evaluated in float128, while the UMFPACK LU stays in double. Newton therefore acts as mixed-precision iterative refinement. It needed exactly as many iterations as in double precision (43 per run on dev1 and 44 on dev2, D6), but its final updates are ≤ 2.2e-34 V instead of ≤ 1.6e-16 V.
- **Cost.** Since the iteration counts are equal, all of the extra cost is per-iteration float128 work: wall time ×10.8–10.9 on the dev1 level-1 mesh (85 378 unknowns) and ×15.6–15.7 on dev2 (273 314 unknowns). Peak RSS is +17% on dev2. The dev1 WP2 runs predate the A9 allocator setting, so their RSS cannot be compared.
- **What remains at the 1e-10 level (D1, post hoc).** Nothing in it points to a DEVSIM error, as far as two biases can tell.
  - A diffusion-limited response, I ∝ 1 − exp(−V_D/V_T), leaves the two-point Richardson value a truncation of R_sinh = −x₁⁴/12000 = −1.87e-10, where x₁ = 1 mV/V_T. The measured second-order coefficients are within 2% of the sinh value 1/6.
  - After subtracting R_sinh, the G_DD residual is ≤ 7.4e-12 in magnitude in five states. At AR-1 it is −4.3e-10, which matches WP2's forms indicator there: the edge-energy form is 4.6e-10 above the second form.
  - The G_SD residuals reach 1.0e-10. That is more than the G_SD forms indicators (≤ 1.1e-13) or the D2 shifts (≤ 4.6e-11) can explain, so the sinh truncation does not describe the source current to that level. I did not pursue this.
  - Two biases cannot measure the fourth-order coefficient, so this is a consistency check, not a decomposition. Either way, everything left is at least 1.6e3 below τ_num.

### What B2 shows

- **DEVSIM's AC command is the WP2 coupled small-signal solve.** In double precision G_AC is bit-identical to WP2's `G_M` in 5 of 6 states (D4). `G_M` is the DEVSIM Jacobian solved with UMFPACK through `devsim.umfpack`. In AR-5 the two differ by −1.7e-4 relative, against an error of +0.55; I did not investigate why.
- **So the P-SS failure in the WP2 atlas is what a DEVSIM user gets from the documented `solve(type='ac')`.** The errors are those WP2 recorded for `G_M`: up to 1.7e3 relative at ψ̂ = 12, and 25 on dev2 (AR-4). They occur on systems with κ_G = 4.9e14 to 1.2e18 and a backward error at machine precision, so a residual check accepts them.
- **Extended precision does not repair it, and was predicted not to** (Y2, from the code path). DEVSIM's `DirectLinearSolver::ACSolveImpl` (`src/math/DirectLinearSolver.cc`) factors and solves the AC system once. In extended mode it is assembled in float128 but converted to double for UMFPACK by `ExternalPreconditioner<float128>`, with no refinement step. The extended-precision error is larger than the double-precision one in 5 of 6 states (ratio ×0.93 to ×1.88); this was not investigated.
- **Unpredicted dev2 states.** The double-precision values are +25, +0.55 and +4.6e-3, as in WP2's `G_M`; the extended ones are +29, +0.59 and +4.3e-3. AR-6 is below the 1e-2 line but still fails τ_num by a factor of more than 4e3 in both precisions.

## For a DEVSIM user (scope: these devices, meshes and DEVSIM 2.11.2)

1. **Do not trust small post-solve terminal currents in double precision.** `get_contact_current` carried an absolute round-off error of 2.4e-12 to 1.8e-11 A/cm on these meshes, so a current or current difference below about 2e-9 A/cm is not accurate to 1%. Either:
   - set `extended_solver`, `extended_model` and `extended_equation`, at ×11–16 wall time; or
   - read the circuit-node current, at no extra cost.

   Both reproduced the reference to ≤ 6.2e-10 here. The circuit-node current also reproduced it to ≤ 1.2e-9 in all 23 double-precision WP2 runs.
2. **Do not take a zero-bias small-signal conductance from `solve(type='ac')` in deep subthreshold.** It is one direct solve of a system with κ_G up to 1e18; it was wrong by up to 3.3e3 relative here, and extended precision does not help. A residual check does not flag it either. Use a ± finite-difference pair with the circuit-node current (or a weighting-function current) instead.

## Deviations and limits

- **Execution deviations:** W4-A1 and W4-A2, above. One finished run was lost to my stopper and repeated; the repeat matches line for line.
- **Export hook:** written in `2462e61` against §6, found before use, and removed (above).
- **Weak priors.** X1–X3 were informed by a single smoke state, and Y1 by WP2's `G_M`. The registered outcomes hold with wide margins: X1 by a factor of at least 1.9e4, X2 by at least 1.6e3.
- **Narrow scope.** One DEVSIM version (2.11.2) on one platform: Linux x86-64, UMFPACK 5.1 with the system BLAS/LAPACK, single-threaded. Level-1 meshes only, six states, one AC frequency. The cost ratios are for these meshes.
- **Not independent.** Every WP4 path is DEVSIM's own output, and the reference equilibrium also comes from DEVSIM. WP4 scores DEVSIM's user-facing output paths; it is not an independent-code comparison, and nothing here is experimental validation.
- **Not explained:** the AR-5 difference between G_AC and `G_M`, and the larger extended-precision AC errors. D1's decomposition rests on an assumed fourth-order coefficient.
